I have been thinking about filling the blanks of a Boolean matrix. More precisely, the starting matrix is in {0,1,b}^{m x n}, where b is a blank entry that still needs a 0 or a 1. We leave the entries already filled alone. The finished matrix should not contain a diagonal rectangle

$$\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 0\\
0 & 1
\end{bmatrix}$$

as a submatrix. We can choose any two rows and any two columns to get this rectangle, keeping their order. An antidiagonal arrangement is not the same forbidden pattern.

It is easy to see how a blank can be forced: if the other three corners are already filled, one value might immediately make a diagonal rectangle, so that value cannot be used. Some matrices fail to have a completion because of restrictions like this. But what happens if there are no such forced blanks at the start? I only mean this direct three-corner restriction. A blank in a rectangle with two filled corners, one filled corner, or no filled corners does not count as forced on that account.

So here is what I want to ask. Given a partially filled matrix with at least one blank, where no blank is forced in this way, can we always fill all the blanks and avoid a diagonal rectangle? Can you turn this into a conjecture and prove or disprove it?
