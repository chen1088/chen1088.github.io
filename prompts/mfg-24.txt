We have a partially filled matrix in {0,1,b}^{m x n}. Each b is a blank to be filled with 0 or 1, and existing entries cannot change. We want no diagonal rectangle

$$\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 0\\
0 & 1
\end{bmatrix}$$

on any two rows and columns, even nonadjacent ones, keeping their order. The antidiagonal is allowed, and the given entries do not already contain the forbidden rectangle.

Sometimes three filled corners force the blank fourth corner: one bit would create that pattern, so the other must be used. Fewer than three other filled corners do not count as forcing a blank here. But suppose we have at least one blank, and trying either 0 or 1 in any single blank, leaving the others blank, never immediately creates a diagonal rectangle. Does a complete filling always exist? Can you formulate the conjecture and prove or disprove it?
