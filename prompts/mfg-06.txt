We are trying to fill a partially filled matrix in {0,1,b}^{m x n}, where b means a blank that we can replace by either 0 or 1. The entries that are already 0 or 1 stay as they are. We want to fill all the blanks without getting a diagonal rectangle

$$\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 0\\
0 & 1
\end{bmatrix}$$

as a submatrix. Here we can pick any two rows and any two columns, but we keep their order. We are excluding this diagonal pattern, not the antidiagonal one. Assume the filled entries do not already contain the displayed pattern.

Sometimes three corners of a rectangle are already filled, and this forces the last corner to be 0 or 1. If we put the other value there, we immediately get the forbidden rectangle. These forced choices can cause trouble, and some partial matrices cannot be completed at all. But I am wondering whether we can still run into trouble when none of the blanks is forced to begin with. A rectangle with only one or two filled corners does not force a blank in this sense; nor does one with no filled corners.

Suppose we have at least one blank, and every blank is free of such an immediate restriction. Can we always finish filling the matrix without a diagonal rectangle? Can you formulate this as a conjecture and prove or disprove it?
