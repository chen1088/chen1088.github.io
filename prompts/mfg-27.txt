Here is something I would like to settle about partially filled matrices. We have a matrix in {0,1,b}^{m x n}, where b marks a blank that still needs either 0 or 1. The filled entries cannot be changed. Our goal is to fill all the blanks without creating a diagonal rectangle

$$\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 0\\
0 & 1
\end{bmatrix}$$

as a submatrix. By a submatrix here I mean any two rows and any two columns, with their order preserved, whether or not they are consecutive. We are only avoiding the displayed pattern, not the antidiagonal one. Also, the given entries do not already make a diagonal rectangle before we start.

There can be blanks whose value is immediately forced. If the other three corners of a rectangle are filled already, one choice at the blank may complete the pattern, so we must use the other choice. Some matrices cannot be filled because of restrictions like this. What I am curious about is the case where we begin with at least one blank, and each blank separately allows both 0 and 1 without either choice making a diagonal rectangle with the given entries. A rectangle with zero, one, or two other corners filled does not force a blank in the sense I am using.

Is there always a way to finish such a matrix? Could you put this question into a conjecture and try to prove it or disprove it?
