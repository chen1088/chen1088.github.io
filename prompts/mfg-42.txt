I was looking at a matrix in {0,1,b}^{m x n}, where b is a blank to be filled with 0 or 1. We keep the given entries and try to avoid

$$\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 0\\
0 & 1
\end{bmatrix}$$

as a submatrix. We can pick any two rows and columns, not necessarily consecutive ones, but preserve their order. The antidiagonal pattern is allowed. The filled entries do not already contain the displayed pattern.

Three filled corners can force the value at a blank fourth corner, since one choice would complete a diagonal rectangle. Such restrictions can make filling impossible. But suppose there is at least one blank, and we can try either 0 or 1 at any one blank without immediately making the pattern from the given entries. Here I only mean an immediate restriction from three other filled corners, not a rectangle with two, one, or zero corners filled.

Can we always finish filling such a matrix without a diagonal rectangle? Could you formulate the conjecture and prove or disprove it?
