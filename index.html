About

I am a PhD student in Computer Science and Engineering at the University at Buffalo, advised by Kenneth Regan.

My research interests include algorithms, complexity, automata theory, and combinatorics. My long term research work is analytic framework of syntactic monoids and their products. My current PhD thesis has a particular focus on the Separating Words Problem: how many states does a DFA need to accept one word and reject another?

Research taste

I developed a taste alongside of my research journey, favoring approaches that are elementary, minimalist, and irregular.

Elementary
I ensure every patient high-school student can understand my proofs.
Minimalist
I try my best to simplify my proofs without compressing them.
Irregular
Favoring irregularity was intensified as I wrote a lot of code to find counterexamples and patterns.

∀01 closure

Champion directed graphs whose 0/1 labelings induce DFA families with the longest shortest identity length H.

Shared vertices stay in place. Click to enlarge.

Definition, legend and data

Each drawing is a rooted directed graph with two outgoing edges at each vertex. Assigning 0 and 1 to those edges independently at each vertex induces its ∀01 closure of DFAs. Each labeling stays fixed throughout a run. An identity u = v is a pair of distinct equal-length words that reach the same state from root 0 in every induced DFA.

One certified champion directed graph at each size, selected and relabeled to keep consecutive drawings close. Vertex labels and positions persist across panels; root 0 stays fixed. All edges use the same style. Redirect counts cover changes to outgoing edges at retained vertices; the new vertex has two additional outgoing edges.
